A token launch radar. It reads the bounty's sealed snapshot of 758 protocols, separates the ones that already record a token from the ones still to come, ranks the pre-token ones by an implied value fitted on the snapshot's own figures and tested out of sample, and prices a farm plan for every ranked protocol.

The separation reads the snapshot's token record whole: a protocol counts as already recording a token when it carries a symbol OR a recorded market cap — 416 of the 758, never ranked. The remaining 342 are screened with the house list's own structural rules — exchange books, bridges, liquid staking and versioned or wrapped products are set aside and counted — leaving the ranked board of 204, watchlist first.

Nothing in the model is asserted. Every candidate term is admitted only when it improves at least three of four held-out tests: hash-parity halves in both directions (fit on one half of the sample, score on the other, then swap), leave-one-out, and walk-forward by listing date; each test prints both the median miss and the share of real caps priced within a factor of two, so a term that nails the median but sprays the middle is visible. The value model prices a launch from the 128 priced comparables — a flat median multiple, then a size curve in log TVL, then a ridge-shrunk family effect survive; 7-day growth, age and chain count do not improve three of the four tests and are printed with their scores, not used. The final model's four tests: ×3.48 (33% within 2×) and ×3.51 (25%) on the two parity directions, ×3.38 (29%) leave-one-out, ×2.92 (34%) walk-forward, against ×5.31 for a flat multiple walk-forward. Each row's printed range is the model's own leave-one-out residual band, and the page publishes how often real caps actually fell inside it — 53% against a stated 50%, 79% against a stated 80%, walk-forward.

The odds model is a ridge logistic regression trained by 1,500 full-batch gradient passes on the 645 dated protocols — outcome: it records a token today — over an age curve in log years and its square, size, growth, chain count and a ridge-shrunk family term, admitted by the same four tests scored on Brier. An age curve, chain count and the family term survive: Brier 0.216 and 0.209 on the two parity directions, 0.211 ten-fold, 0.225 walk-forward, against 0.251 and 0.318 for the base rate alone. A row's odds are the model's probability that a protocol with its figures, still tokenless at its age, records a token within the next 24 months, printed next to the raw record of its closest peers. Implied value = value if it launches × those odds. A random control term is fitted in both models and never admitted — its row is printed so the kept terms can be judged against the noise floor.

Farm plans are written to be followed, not admired. Every route names its bridge — the Base Bridge from Ethereum to Base, the Arbitrum Bridge, OP Mainnet's own bridge, Circle CCTP for native USDC, the Wormhole Portal for Solana, and so on — with a cost and a time on every step, a weekly cadence checklist, a pre-exit review two weeks out (points-programme status, withdrawal terms, curator and performance fees, a TVL exit trigger), and an exit rule. Each plan prints its own sizing chain with the row's real numbers: expected drop = implied value × 7.5% drop share × deposit ÷ TVL, minus the plan's fees, over its hours, against a $25-an-hour line — FARM above it, THIN when the plan merely pays for itself, SKIP when it loses money. The dashboard's controls re-price every verdict live under other deposits, drop shares and horizons.

The dashboard is a dark, offline page: a freshness strip that states the sealed snapshot's date, protocol count and checksum and the radar's published weekly refresh schedule — a re-scan and re-price every Monday 00:00 UTC, with a live countdown to the next one — KPI evidence tiles, the full protocol accounting (all 758 in exactly one group), self-check chips the page recomputes as it renders (row count, rank order, totals, a checksum against the program's own), the four-test evidence tables, a sortable and filterable board with expandable six-step farm plans, a detail view per protocol whose farm plan is shown whole, CSV/JSON export, and deep links (#p=&lt;slug&gt;, #q=&lt;text&gt;, #cat=&lt;category&gt;, #w=1). A dense board view (#dense=1) puts the whole watchlist and the ranked field beyond it — sixty-four protocols, priced and verdicted — on one frame, because a radar's claim is coverage and the board should show it; #dense=1&w=1 is the same frame under the watchlist filter. In v7 that frame also carries the evidence on its face: above the table sit six headline cards (the walk-forward miss, the range's honesty, the odds Brier, the terms kept, the implied total recomputed in the page against the program's checksum), the full 758-protocol accounting as a stacked bar with its legend, and the two held-out ladders — every value and odds term with its leave-one-out and walk-forward scores and its verdict — the proof the full deck below holds, no longer hidden from the board's own screenshot — and the frame fits the gate's measure, no side past 1440 px. The model is unchanged from v5 and v6 — same sealed snapshot, same fits, same printed figures; v7 changes only what the board's first frame shows. The program is deterministic — fits, folds and bootstrap refits are pure functions of the snapshot's bytes, so two runs write the same board — and it writes its fitted evidence into the dashboard folder as evidence.js, so the numbers the page prints are part of the handed-in source.

## Run it

```sh
mkdir -p out && MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=out node main.ts --input ../input/snapshot.json
```
